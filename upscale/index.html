From the reel · you commented UPSCALE

Make small photos big and sharp, for free

Here's everything from the reel. The app is Upscayl, a free, open-source AI upscaler made by the Upscayl team. It takes a small or pixelated photo and rebuilds it at up to 4 times the size, right on your own computer. No account, no subscription, no uploads.

What you need

  • A computer with Windows 10 or later, macOS 12 or later, or Linux.
  • A graphics card that supports Vulkan. Most gaming and laptop GPUs from the last few years do. Many built-in (integrated) graphics don't, but it costs nothing to try.

Install it

  1. Open upscayl.org (or the GitHub Releases page) and download the -win.exe file.
  2. Double-click it. If Windows shows a blue SmartScreen box, click More info → Run anyway.
  3. Follow the installer, then open Upscayl.
  1. Download the -mac.dmg file from upscayl.org.
  2. Open it and drag Upscayl into your Applications folder.
  3. The first time, right-click Upscayl in Applications and choose Open, then Open again.
  1. Look for Upscayl in your software store (Flatpak or Snap), or download the .AppImage from upscayl.org.
  2. For the AppImage: right-click it → Properties → Permissions → allow it to run as a program, then double-click it.

Use it

  1. Click Select Image and pick your photo.
  2. Choose a model. General Photo is the best place to start; Digital Art suits drawings and screenshots.
  3. Pick an output folder, then press Upscayl and give it a moment.
  4. Got a whole folder? Switch on Batch Upscayl and select the folder instead of one image.

Mac shortcut (Homebrew)

brew install --cask upscayl

Only if you already use Homebrew. Otherwise the download above is easiest.

What you get

Up to 4× bigger

Small photos come out larger with rebuilt detail.

Batch mode

Upscale a whole folder in one go.

Several AI models

For photos, art and more, plus extra models you can add later.

Private by design

Everything runs on your machine. Your photos never get uploaded.

Good to know

  • It works best on low-resolution or pixelated photos. It can't fix a photo that is out of focus or motion-blurred.
  • No compatible graphics card? It may not run at all. Their Troubleshooting page on GitHub has a few workarounds.
